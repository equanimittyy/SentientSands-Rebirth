Write a radiant conversation: a talk between the characters below. They travel together in the player's group, and they stand near each other. The TOPIC starts the talk, and the talk can move on from it, as a real conversation does.

PLACE: {place}

CHARACTERS:

HOW WELL THEY KNOW EACH OTHER:
{acquaintance}

TOPIC: {topic}

LINES: {length}

RULES:
- The conversation has a beginning, an exchange, and an end. One character opens it with a remark, a question, or a thing that it notices. The others take it up, and the talk goes back and forth. The last line closes it: an answer that settles it, a joke, a shrug, a curt word that cuts it off, or a call to move on. The conversation never stops on a question that nobody answers.
- A character speaks only when it has something to say, so not every character has to speak. In a short conversation, some of them only listen.
- Write each line as Name|ID: words, with the exact Name|ID of its speaker from CHARACTERS. Each line is one turn.
- Only the characters in CHARACTERS speak. Write no narration and no bracketed text.
- The characters talk to each other, not to the player. Each line follows from what was said before it, so the conversation is an exchange, not a row of separate remarks on the topic. A line can agree, disagree, answer, ask, tease, or turn the talk, and it can be only a few words.
- A character can ask another what it thinks, what it saw, or what it did before. The one asked answers, dodges, or brushes it off, and its answer can lead the talk somewhere new. The characters use each other's names only where people do: to catch the attention of one in the group, or to answer one of them.
- The order of the speakers follows the talk, not a fixed round: the one asked or teased speaks next, two characters can trade lines while the others listen, and no character speaks twice in a row.
- Characters that know each other little are curious about each other, when their personality allows it. Characters that talked often skip the introductions and bring up what they know of each other.
- Each character takes the topic in its own way, from its personality, its past, and its own concerns, and says what it thinks, so the characters do not have to agree.
- Each character sounds like no one else: its manner of speech decides its words, its rhythm, and the length of its lines. Two characters never share a turn of phrase or a kind of image.

Output ONLY the lines.
